1. Term Frequency (TF)

TF(t, d) = 1 + log(count(t, d))

Measures how frequently term t occurs in document d.

2. Inverse Document Frequency (IDF)

IDF(t) = log((1 + N) / (1 + DF(t))) + 1

Weights down common general words and boosts unique examination terms.

3. Cosine Similarity Metric

CosineSim(u, v) = (u · v) / (||u|| * ||v||)

Computes the geometric angle between normalized vector spaces regardless of document length.

Viva Voce Q&A Preparation

10 common external examiner questions with concise student answers

Q1: What is the main objective of this College FAQ Chatbot?

Answer: To provide a lightweight, responsive, and offline AI-driven assistant that answers student queries regarding semester examinations, hall tickets, results, and rules using NLP instead of rigid keyword matching.

Q2: Why did you choose TF-IDF + Cosine Similarity over simple if-else matching?

Answer: If-else statements fail if the student writes variations (e.g. "when are sem exams?" vs "tell me exam dates"). TF-IDF maps phrases into a vector space where cosine similarity measures semantic closeness, allowing flexible natural phrasing.

Q3: What preprocessing steps are applied to the user's input?

Answer: Lowercasing, punctuation stripping, domain slang/abbreviation expansion (e.g., 'sem' → 'semester', 'reval' → 'revaluation'), tokenization, and stopword removal.

Q4: How does TF-IDF calculate word importance?

Answer: TF (Term Frequency) measures how often a word appears in a query, while IDF (Inverse Document Frequency) reduces the weight of ubiquitous words and gives high weights to discriminative words like 'revaluation' or 'calculator'.

Q5: What is Cosine Similarity and what is its range?

Answer: It is the dot product of two normalized vectors, representing the cosine of the angle between them. Its value ranges from 0 (completely orthogonal/unrelated) to 1 (identical vector direction).

Q6: How do you handle out-of-scope or gibberish questions?

Answer: We enforce a confidence threshold (0.22). If the maximum cosine similarity across all patterns is below this threshold, a graceful fallback message is triggered prompting the student with supported exam topics.

Q7: What is the purpose of N-grams in your vectorizer?

Answer: We use unigrams and bigrams (1, 2) to preserve word pairings such as "hall ticket", "internal exam", and "passing criteria", which have distinct meanings compared to isolated individual words.

Q8: Does this application require an active internet connection or paid API key?

Answer: No. The system runs completely locally and offline using Python Flask and Scikit-Learn without depending on OpenAI, Gemini, or any paid cloud APIs.

Q9: How is the FAQ dataset structured?

Answer: The dataset is stored as a JSON object containing 32 distinct examination intents, each with an intent name, category, multiple training patterns/variations, a verified response, and contextual quick suggestion chips.

Q10: What are the limitations and future enhancements?

Answer: Current limitations include closed-domain coverage and no multi-turn context memory. Future enhancements include integrating BERT/Sentence-Transformers, multi-language support, and direct integration with college ERP databases for live personal grades.
